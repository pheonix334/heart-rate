How this calculator works

This is a simplified, transparent point-based estimate — not a clinical risk score. Starting from your actual age, it adds or subtracts years based on well-established cardiovascular risk factors, then shows the resulting "heart age" alongside a breakdown of exactly what added or subtracted years and by how much.

Frequently asked questions

Heart age is an illustrative way of expressing cardiovascular risk factors as an age comparison — if your heart age is higher than your actual age, it means your combined risk factors are similar to what's typically seen in an older person with fewer risk factors, not that your heart is literally aging faster in a measurable sense.
No. Clinical tools like the Framingham Risk Score or QRISK use validated statistical models built on large population studies with exact lab values (like precise cholesterol numbers) and produce a calibrated percentage risk of a cardiac event. This calculator is a simplified, transparent, educational estimate using broader categories — it's meant to illustrate which factors matter, not to replace a clinical assessment.
In this model, diabetes and smoking carry the largest single impact, followed by high blood pressure and high cholesterol. Combined factors compound — someone with two or three risk factors sees a larger cumulative effect than any one alone.
Many of the factors here are modifiable: quitting smoking, increasing physical activity, managing blood pressure and cholesterol (through diet, medication, or both), and reaching a healthier weight can all move the estimate in a better direction over time — and more importantly, reflect real reductions in cardiovascular risk.
Treat it as a prompt to talk with a doctor, not a diagnosis. A large gap usually reflects multiple risk factors that are worth a real clinical evaluation, which can look at your actual lab values and personal/family history in full.
There's no strict schedule — revisiting it after a health checkup (when you have updated blood pressure or cholesterol numbers) or after a meaningful lifestyle change is a reasonable approach.
Yes — after calculating, tap "Save this result" to add it to your private history, stored on your device only. A simple trend chart shows how your heart age has moved as your health factors and lifestyle change.
Smoking and diabetes carry the largest point values, followed by Stage 2 blood pressure and high cholesterol. The full breakdown table above shows exactly how many years each factor adds or subtracts.
Not a diagnostic or clinical tool: This calculator uses a simplified, illustrative point system and self-reported inputs (not lab values). It does not replace a cardiovascular risk assessment from a healthcare provider. If you have concerns about your heart health, please consult a doctor.
